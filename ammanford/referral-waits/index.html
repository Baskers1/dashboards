What the numbers mean. These are open referral to treatment (RTT) pathways at the end of the month: the waiting list. Median wait is how long those pathways have waited so far, not how long a new referral will wait. "Within 26 weeks" is the share that have waited 26 weeks or less. Wales measures waits against 26 weeks (target 95%) and 36 weeks (target: nobody waiting longer), not England's 18 weeks. StatsWales publishes waits in one-week bands, so medians here are worked out from those bands and are accurate to within a week. Pathways are not the same as patients: some people have more than one open pathway. Care type groups are my own grouping of NHS Wales specialties.

Site level and referral. StatsWales publishes these figures per health board (the board providing the care), not per hospital. Hywel Dda's figures cover Glangwili (Carmarthen), Prince Philip (Llanelli), Withybush (Haverfordwest), Bronglais (Aberystwyth) and its community hospitals, including Amman Valley in Glanamman, together. Wales has no equivalent of England's Right to Choose: a GP in Ammanford normally refers within Hywel Dda, and treatment in another board goes through regional or commissioned arrangements (many specialist services for the area, such as cardiac surgery and plastic surgery, are already provided at Morriston in Swansea Bay). The figures help you ask your GP an informed question; they do not mean you can book with another board yourself.

No ratings in Wales. Healthcare Inspectorate Wales (HIW) inspects NHS hospitals in Wales, usually one ward or service at a time, and publishes a report with findings and an improvement plan. It does not give an overall rating like CQC's Outstanding to Inadequate. The nearest thing to a board-wide judgement is the Welsh Government's escalation level, agreed with HIW and Audit Wales, which is shown above.

Reading it. The Welsh targets are that 95% of patients spend under 4 hours in an emergency department and nobody spends 12 hours or more. Minor injury units see less complex cases and nearly always meet 4 hours, so board totals look better than the major departments alone: switch to "major emergency departments only" to compare like with like. Prince Philip in Llanelli, the nearest hospital to Ammanford, runs a minor injury unit rather than a full emergency department; the nearest major departments are Glangwili in Carmarthen and Morriston in Swansea.

ONS, Deaths registered in England and Wales, 2025 edition (released 25 September 2026): Table 5 (cause, age and place of death, England and Wales together) and Table 6 (place of death by area of usual residence). Years are year of registration, 2016 to 2025, extended back to 2010-2014 using earlier ONS tables (2015 and 2006-2009 are not available). Before 2016, breakdowns are only available for Wales as a whole, not by health board. Ammanford sits in Hywel Dda, so that is the default area, compared with Wales.

Institution here means type of place, not a named hospital. ONS records where a death occurred (NHS hospitals, care homes, hospices, home and so on). It does not name individual hospitals. The area is where the person usually lived, not where they died.

Why there is no hospital mortality chart. The England version of this page shows the SHMI, which NHS England publishes for English trusts only. Wales has no equivalent published as open data for each hospital, so it is left out rather than estimated.
