About this data

High (H) and low (L) water at Devonport, Plymouth, height in metres, times already adjusted for BST. Plymstock and Saltash use the same table. Sunrise and sunset shown for planning daylight. Tables: tidetimes.org.uk (derived in part from UK Hydrographic Office data; personal use). The curve is a smooth estimate drawn between the listed high and low waters, and shaded areas are night. Weather and wind can shift tide times and heights a little.

Bathing Water Quality

    Live Sewage Alerts

    About this data and live map

    Surfers Against Sewage run a near-real-time map of sewage discharge alerts for about 850 UK swim spots. Check it for Plymouth Hoe, Bovisand and Wembury before anyone paddles or swims.

    Open the live sewage mapWhat the alerts meanStorm overflow map (sewagedata.co.uk)
    • No alert: data is coming in and there is no sign of reduced water quality.
    • Sewage alert: a confirmed discharge, which stays active for 48 hours after it stops.
    • Pollution risk forecast: bathing discouraged; bathing season only.
    • Pollution incident: confirmed event such as a burst pipe or oil spill; bathing not advised.
    • Out of season: no bathing-water data outside the season, which ended on 30 September.

    This page can't show Surfers Against Sewage's live data itself: they don't publish a data feed or reuse licence, and the map can't be embedded here. Alert wording is summarised from Surfers Against Sewage's Data HQ page.

    Walk Ideas

    About these walks

    Distances and times are approximate, and the links go to other websites whose routes may differ a little from the ones described here. Bus routes and timetables change: check Plymouth Citybus and Stagecoach South West before setting off, and take a phone and water. On Dartmoor, weather changes fast and mist can come down quickly, so take a map and a warm layer. The small tiles beside each walk are simple illustrations of its type (coast or inland), not photographs of the route.
