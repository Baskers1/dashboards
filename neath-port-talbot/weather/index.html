River Levels

About this data

Latest readings from Natural Resources Wales river level stations (Open Government Licence v3.0), with NRW's own rising, steady or falling trend. Each station reports on its own scale in metres, so levels cannot be compared between stations. The usual range (shaded band, where known) is the range the level is in 90% of the time, as published by RiverLevels.uk from NRW data. The line builds up from the readings collected each time this page is refreshed. The Neath, Dulais, Clydach and Afan rise quickly after heavy rain on the hills above the valleys. This is a level reading, not a flood forecast. Check NRW flood warnings for official warnings and NRW river levels for full charts.

Walk Ideas

About these walks

Walks are listed by distance from the place you have chosen, nearest first. Distances and times are approximate, and the links go to other websites whose routes may differ a little from the ones described here. Times marked "estimate" are worked out from the distance because the source gives none, and "Not graded" means the source gives no difficulty. Check Traveline Cymru for bus and train times before setting off. Margam Country Park's paths, apart from public rights of way, are permissive and the park may close them from time to time. The small tiles beside each walk are simple illustrations of its type (waterside or fields and hills), not photographs of the route.

Sources: Visit Wales, Dramatic Heart of Wales, Neath Port Talbot Council, Wales Coast Path, Margam Country Park and Active Traveller. Many more routes, with maps and reviews, are on AllTrails.

Prefer to walk with others? The Neath Port Talbot Ramblers lead regular group walks; their programme is on the group's website.
