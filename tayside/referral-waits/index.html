What the numbers mean. These are ongoing waits: patients still on the list at the end of the month. Median wait is how long they have waited so far, not how long a new referral will wait. "Within 12 weeks" is the share who have waited 12 weeks or less. Scotland's standards are that 95% of patients wait no more than 12 weeks for a new outpatient appointment, and that inpatient and day case treatment starts within 12 weeks of agreeing it (the Treatment Time Guarantee). PHS publishes medians in days; they are shown here in weeks. Care type groups are my own grouping of NHS Scotland specialties.

Hospital names link to the HIS report. A blank count means the report summary gives no figure.

No ratings in Scotland. Healthcare Improvement Scotland (HIS) inspects NHS hospitals, but it does not give an overall rating like CQC's Outstanding to Inadequate. Each report lists areas of good practice, recommendations (things the board should do) and requirements (things it must do to meet standards). More requirements usually means more problems found, but counts are not comparable between a full inspection and a follow-up, or between acute and maternity inspections.

Reading it. Minor injury units see less complex cases and nearly always meet 4 hours, so board totals are best compared alongside the emergency department rows. Waits over 8 and 12 hours are only counted at sites that send patient-level data.

Reading it carefully. The HSMR compares deaths within 30 days of admission with the number predicted from each hospital's patients (age, sex, deprivation, diagnosis, admission type and so on). PHS presents it as a screening tool, not a measure of avoidable deaths or quality of care. The control limits here are worked out as 1 ± 3/√predicted deaths, close to the funnel plot PHS publishes, so a hospital near a limit should be checked against the PHS dashboard.
