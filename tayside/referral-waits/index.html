What the numbers mean. These are ongoing waits: patients still on the list at the end of the month. Median wait is how long they have waited so far, not how long a new referral will wait. "Within 12 weeks" is the share who have waited 12 weeks or less. Scotland's standards are that 95% of patients wait no more than 12 weeks for a new outpatient appointment, and that inpatient and day case treatment starts within 12 weeks of agreeing it (the Treatment Time Guarantee). PHS publishes medians in days; they are shown here in weeks. Care type groups are my own grouping of NHS Scotland specialties.

Site level and referral. PHS publishes these figures per NHS board, not per hospital. Tayside's figures cover Ninewells, Perth Royal Infirmary, Stracathro and its community hospitals together. Since October 2025 waits are counted against the board where the patient will be seen, and treatment bought from the independent sector is reported separately. NHS Scotland has no equivalent of England's Right to Choose: a referral to another board goes through the usual cross-boundary arrangements. Some national services, such as the Golden Jubilee in Clydebank, take referrals from every board and are not shown here.

No ratings in Scotland. Healthcare Improvement Scotland (HIS) inspects NHS hospitals, but it does not give an overall rating like CQC's Outstanding to Inadequate. Each report lists areas of good practice, recommendations (things the board should do) and requirements (things it must do to meet standards). More requirements usually means more problems found, but counts are not comparable between a full inspection and a follow-up, or between acute and maternity inspections.

Reading it carefully. The HSMR compares deaths within 30 days of admission with the number predicted from each hospital's patients (age, sex, deprivation, diagnosis, admission type and so on). PHS presents it as a screening tool, not a measure of avoidable deaths or quality of care. The control limits here are worked out as 1 ± 3/√predicted deaths, close to the funnel plot PHS publishes, so a hospital near a limit should be checked against the PHS dashboard. Stracathro mainly does planned day surgery, so its numbers are too small to read anything into.

What this measures. For everyone who died in the year (excluding deaths from external causes such as accidents), PHS links NRS death registrations to hospital records and works out how much of their last six months was spent outside hospital: at home, in a care home or hospice, or elsewhere in the community. It is the measure Scotland uses in place of a place-of-death breakdown by area. The area is where the person lived, not where they died. The 2020/21 rise reflects the pandemic. The latest year is provisional.
